# AP Statistics Curriculum Reference

Aligned to the College Board AP Statistics Course and Exam Description (CED).

## Unit 1: Exploring One-Variable Data
- Categorical data: bar charts, pie charts, frequency tables
- Quantitative data: dotplots, histograms, stemplots, boxplots
- Describing distributions: shape, center, spread, outliers (SOCS)
- Measures of center: mean, median
- Measures of spread: range, IQR, standard deviation
- Outliers and the 1.5×IQR rule
- Normal distributions, empirical rule, z-scores

## Unit 2: Exploring Two-Variable Data
- Scatterplots and describing associations (DUFS: direction, unusual features, form, strength)
- Correlation coefficient r
- Least-squares regression line (LSRL)
- Residuals and residual plots
- Interpreting slope and y-intercept in context
- r² (coefficient of determination)
- Influential points, outliers, leverage
- Categorical data relationships: two-way tables, marginal/conditional distributions

## Unit 3: Collecting Data
- Census vs. sample vs. experiment vs. observational study
- Sampling methods: SRS, stratified, cluster, systematic, convenience
- Sources of bias: response, nonresponse, undercoverage, voluntary response, question wording
- Experiments: treatments, factors, levels, response variable, experimental units
- Principles of experimental design: control, randomization, replication
- Blocking and matched pairs
- Scope of inference: random sampling → generalizability, random assignment → causation

## Unit 4: Probability, Random Variables, and Probability Distributions
- Basic probability rules, complement, addition, multiplication
- Conditional probability and independence
- Discrete random variables: mean (expected value) and standard deviation
- Combining random variables (linear transformations, sums)
- Binomial distributions
- Geometric distributions

## Unit 5: Sampling Distributions
- Sampling distribution of a sample proportion $\hat{p}$
- Sampling distribution of a sample mean $\bar{x}$
- Central Limit Theorem
- Conditions: independence (10% condition) and normality (large counts / large n)
- Mean and standard deviation of sampling distributions

## Unit 6: Inference for Categorical Data — Proportions
- Confidence interval for one proportion
- Hypothesis test for one proportion (one-sample z-test)
- Confidence interval for difference of two proportions
- Hypothesis test for difference of two proportions (two-sample z-test)
- Type I and Type II errors, power

## Unit 7: Inference for Quantitative Data — Means
- Confidence interval for one mean (one-sample t-interval)
- Hypothesis test for one mean (one-sample t-test)
- Matched pairs t-test
- Confidence interval for difference of two means (two-sample t-interval)
- Hypothesis test for difference of two means (two-sample t-test)

## Unit 8: Inference for Categorical Data — Chi-Square
- Chi-square goodness-of-fit test
- Chi-square test for homogeneity
- Chi-square test for independence
- Expected counts, degrees of freedom, conditions

## Unit 9: Inference for Quantitative Data — Slopes
- Confidence interval for the slope of a regression line (t-interval)
- Hypothesis test for the slope of a regression line (t-test)
- Computer output interpretation

## AP Exam Format
- **Multiple Choice**: 40 questions, 90 minutes (50% of score)
- **Free Response**: 6 questions, 90 minutes (50% of score)
  - FRQ 1-5: short answer (roughly 12 min each)
  - FRQ 6: investigative task (roughly 25 min)

## Four-Step Inference Framework (AP Exam Standard)
1. **State**: Define parameter, state hypotheses (or confidence level), identify the procedure
2. **Plan**: Name the procedure, check conditions
3. **Do**: Show the math — test statistic and p-value (or interval calculation)
4. **Conclude**: Interpret in context — link back to the original question
